| #     | Problem identified by lecturer                                                           | Her reasoning                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          | Suggested improvement                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ----- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1** | **Diegetic companion feedback is less useful than the non-diegetic feedback.**           | The current platforming challenges are very simple. If the player falls into a gap, they already know they were supposed to jump. Watching the companion jump over the gap does not explain what the player specifically did wrong. Meanwhile, text such as **“You jumped too late”** gives a clear cause of failure. This means the non-diegetic condition may currently provide more useful information than the diegetic condition. | Either make the platforming challenges more complex so that watching the companion is genuinely useful, or make the companion demonstration more informative. For example, if the player jumped too late, the companion should clearly demonstrate an **earlier jump at the correct point**. Both conditions should communicate the same corrective information. |
| **2** | **The statement “the player reacts physically to the failure” is unclear.**              | It is unclear whether “player” refers to the actual participant or the player character in the game. It is also not obvious from the beta demonstration how the physical reaction differs between the two conditions.                                                                                                                                                                                                                  | Clearly define what this means in the project description. If the player character performs an animation after failure, state this explicitly and ensure it is visible. If this feature has been removed, remove it from the project description.                                                                                                                |
| **3** | **Moving-platform feedback is not demonstrating the correct action properly.**           | The companion appears to glide across the screen instead of actually interacting with the moving platform. The arrows also move across the screen without necessarily showing the player exactly what they should do. Therefore, the feedback does not demonstrate the mechanics required to complete the obstacle.                                                                                                                    | Improve the animations. The companion should physically land on the moving platform, move with it, wait where necessary, and jump from it at the correct moment. Arrows should show meaningful information such as where to stand, when to jump, and where to move.                                                                                              |
| **4** | **Some non-diegetic feedback is too obvious.**                                           | Messages such as **“You hit the spikes, jump over them”** do not provide useful information because players already understand that spikes should be avoided. The important information is not what the obstacle is, but why the player failed to avoid it.                                                                                                                                                                            | Replace obvious hazard-based feedback with specific corrective feedback such as **“You jumped too late,” “You jumped too early,” “You did not jump,”** or **“Wait for the platform before jumping.”**                                                                                                                                                            |
| **5** | **The system may not be detecting what the player actually did before failing.**         | The same obstacle can be failed in different ways. For example, a player may fall into a gap because they jumped too early, too late, or did not jump at all. The lecturer noticed an example where feedback described a bad jump even though the player had not actually jumped. This makes the feedback inaccurate.                                                                                                                  | Track the player's actions immediately before failure. Determine whether they jumped, when they jumped, and where the jump occurred. Different mistakes should trigger different feedback.                                                                                                                                                                       |
| **6** | **Feedback currently focuses too much on the obstacle instead of the player's mistake.** | Knowing that the player hit spikes or fell into a gap does not necessarily explain what they need to change on their next attempt. The player needs feedback that answers **“What did I do wrong?”**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   | Change the feedback logic from **hazard detected → generic message** to **failure detected → inspect player actions → determine likely mistake → provide corrective feedback**.                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| **7** | **The two experimental conditions may not currently be equivalent.**                     | If the non-diegetic condition explains the precise reason for failure while the diegetic condition only demonstrates the correct action, then participants are not receiving the same information. Any preference for one condition could therefore be caused by the quality or amount of information rather than by the feedback modality itself.                                                                                     | Ensure both conditions communicate the **same underlying corrective information**, with only the presentation changing. For example, “You jumped too late” in the non-diegetic condition should correspond to the companion demonstrating an earlier correctly timed jump in the diegetic condition.                                                             |
