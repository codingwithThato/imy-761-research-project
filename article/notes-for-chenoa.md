## Suggested Methods text: data analysis

> Each participant's questionnaire responses were averaged into three subscale scores: competence satisfaction (BANGS items 1, 3 and 5; scale 1–7) and competence frustration (BANGS items 2, 4 and 6; scale 1–7), which were kept as separate constructs as recommended by Ballou et al. (2024), and tension/annoyance (GEQ items 7, 8 and 9; scale 0–4; IJsselsteijn et al., 2013). Each subscale was checked for normality within each condition, and the appropriate between-group test was chosen on that basis (see Results). Because there were only five participants per group, all tests were treated as exploratory.
>
> The interview transcripts were analysed using Braun and Clarke's (2006) reflexive thematic analysis. After familiarisation with the data, segments relating to failure, understanding of the feedback, competence and motivation were coded inductively. The codes were grouped into candidate themes, which were reviewed against the full dataset and then defined and named. Finally, the themes were compared between the two conditions.

## For Limitations (worth including)

- **Partial transcripts:** only fragments of the P7 and P9 interviews (both diegetic) were recovered, so the diegetic group's accounts rest more heavily on P1, P2 and P5.
- **P10's transcript:** P10 played the non-diegetic version, but parts of the transcript describe the companion demonstrating jumps, which only happens in the diegetic version. P10 was therefore quoted only on points that don't depend on condition (the "well done" message). Decide together whether to mention this.
- **Shapiro-Wilk with n = 5** has little power to detect non-normality, so passing the test doesn't prove the data are normal.
- **Power:** with n = 5 per group, a two-tailed t-test has 80% power only for very large effects (d ≈ 2.0), and about 8% power for the observed tension/annoyance effect (d = −0.36).
- **Observation notes:** these were taken in 7 of 10 sessions but were not used as data (the lecturer advised against it in D7 because observation was never planned as a method). Mentioning this is optional.
- **Allocation** was not randomised (participants were allocated as they became available).
