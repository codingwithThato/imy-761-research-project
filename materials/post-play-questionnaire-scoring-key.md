# Post-Play Questionnaire — Scoring Key (researcher use only)

Maps each item on the participant-facing `post-play-questionnaire.md` back to its
source instrument and construct. Do not hand this to participants.

| # on handout | Item text | Instrument | Construct | Scale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | I felt competent at the game. | BANGS (Ballou et al., 2024) | Competence satisfaction | 1–7 |
| 2 | I felt incapable while playing. | BANGS (Ballou et al., 2024) | Competence frustration | 1–7 |
| 3 | I successfully completed challenges in the game. | BANGS (Ballou et al., 2024) | Competence satisfaction | 1–7 |
| 4 | I felt like a failure when I played. | BANGS (Ballou et al., 2024) | Competence frustration | 1–7 |
| 5 | I felt capable while playing. | BANGS (Ballou et al., 2024) | Competence satisfaction | 1–7 |
| 6 | The game made me doubt my ability. | BANGS (Ballou et al., 2024) | Competence frustration | 1–7 |
| 7 | I felt frustrated. | GEQ (IJsselsteijn et al., 2013) | Tension/annoyance | 0–4 |
| 8 | I felt annoyed. | GEQ (IJsselsteijn et al., 2013) | Tension/annoyance | 0–4 |
| 9 | I felt irritable. | GEQ (IJsselsteijn et al., 2013) | Tension/annoyance | 0–4 |

## Scoring

- **Competence satisfaction** = mean of items 1, 3, 5.
- **Competence frustration** = mean of items 2, 4, 6.
- **Tension/annoyance (frustration)** = mean of items 7, 8, 9.

Competence satisfaction and competence frustration are scored and reported as two
distinct constructs, consistent with Deliverable 4's rationale for using BANGS
(it treats them as separate dimensions rather than two ends of one scale) — do not
reverse-code and collapse them into a single "competence" number.

## Why the handout looks different from Deliverable 4's Appendix B.2

Deliverable 4's appendix names both instruments (BANGS, GEQ), cites them, and
groups items under subscale headers ("BANGS – Competence frustration", etc.) —
appropriate for an academic deliverable, but not for a participant-facing form.
Two problems with putting that framing in front of an actual participant:

1. It is unnecessary academic apparatus a participant doesn't need to answer the
   items, and it's not age-appropriate — a younger participant (e.g. a 13-year-old
   recruited as "friends and family") shouldn't need to parse citation-style
   instrument names to fill out a 9-item form.
2. The subscale headers effectively label which items are the "positive" ones and
   which are the "negative" ones right above each block, which can shape how
   someone answers the next few items.

The handout keeps the validated item wording verbatim (that's what preserves the
instruments' validity) and keeps the two response scales separate (they're
genuinely different ranges, 1–7 vs 0–4, so can't be freely interleaved without
confusing the answer grid) — it only strips the instrument names, citations, and
subscale labels, and interleaves competence-satisfaction/frustration items instead
of block-grouping them.
